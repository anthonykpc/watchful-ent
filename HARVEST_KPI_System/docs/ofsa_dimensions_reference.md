# OFSA Six Dimensions of Food Security — Reference and Mapping Rationale

This file defines the six dimensions of food security used by the
**New Jersey Office of the Food Security Advocate (OFSA)** and explains
how HARVEST KPIs are tagged to them. Every metric in the KPI catalog
carries one or more of these tags. Tagging is what bridges day-to-day
operational metrics to the OFSA logic-model phase that will follow.

A KPI can (and often should) tag to **multiple** dimensions when it
genuinely touches more than one — e.g., a kitchen rental hour by a
member entrepreneur supports both **Availability** (food being produced
locally) and **Agency** (the entrepreneur exercising economic agency in
the food system).

---

## The Six Dimensions

### 1. Availability

**Definition.** The physical presence of food in sufficient quantity and
appropriate quality from local production, imports, or food assistance.
"Is there food to be had?"

**At HARVEST this means.** Local production volume routed through the
kitchen, produce sourced from UAC-affiliated farms, prepared meals
distributed, and the kitchen's operational capacity itself
(hours available, equipment uptime).

**Typical KPI tags.**
- lbs of produce moved by UAC farmers
- # of HARVEST-produced items in institutional menus
- Kitchen hours rented (proxy for production capacity utilized)
- Food captured for nutritional demos (lbs)

---

### 2. Access

**Definition.** Economic and physical access to food. Affordability,
proximity, transportation, and the social/cultural ability to obtain it.
"Can people reach the food that exists?"

**At HARVEST this means.** Pathways HARVEST creates from food
production to community plates — institutional purchasing that brings
healthy food into healthcare cafeterias, food recovery to direct
service providers (Bridges, etc.), distribution geography for UAC
produce, and the affordability of HARVEST-supported products.

**Typical KPI tags.**
- $ purchasing orders from HARVEST/UAC vendors by institutions
- Where produce was distributed (geography)
- Food donated to Bridges and other service providers (lbs)
- # of HARVEST-produced items in institutional menus

---

### 3. Utilization

**Definition.** The ability to use food appropriately — adequate diet,
clean water, sanitation, food safety, nutritional knowledge, and care
practices that lead to good nutrition. "Is the food actually nourishing
people?"

**At HARVEST this means.** Food safety training (ServSafe), nutrition
education, wellness coaching, teaching kitchen programming with RDs,
and demonstrations that show community members how to prepare
nutritionally dense food from local ingredients.

**Typical KPI tags.**
- % of members achieving ServSafe through HARVEST
- # of participants in teaching kitchen / RD workshops
- # of businesses receiving wellness coaching
- Food captured for nutritional/wellness demos
- Survey metrics from events (learning outcomes)

---

### 4. Stability

**Definition.** The continuity of availability, access, and utilization
over time — resilience against shocks (weather, economic, supply chain)
and the durability of the food system's institutions and relationships.
"Will this still work next year?"

**At HARVEST this means.** Longitudinal business growth of members,
recurring institutional purchasing relationships, sustained UAC
partnership (JSC cadence), the durability of grants and earned revenue
streams, and the operational maturity of HARVEST itself.

**Typical KPI tags.**
- Growth of businesses by hours rented (longitudinal)
- Average % increase in sales for members
- # of grants and partnerships secured
- % earned revenue
- # of JSC meetings held (governance continuity)
- # of CAB members and meetings (governance continuity)

---

### 5. Agency

**Definition.** The capacity of individuals and communities to make
their own decisions about what food they produce, how it is produced,
processed, and distributed, and what they consume. "Who gets to
decide?"

**At HARVEST this means.** Entrepreneur ownership of their businesses,
representation of underrepresented food entrepreneurs, multilingual and
culturally relevant programming, the CAB's role in shaping HARVEST,
and the visibility / showcasing of member businesses to wider
audiences.

**Typical KPI tags.**
- # of entrepreneurs reached and converted to renting members
- # of businesses supported (especially demographic breakdowns)
- # of multilingual/cultural events
- # of events showcasing local entrepreneurs
- # of CAB members across sectors
- Farmer demographics

---

### 6. Sustainability

**Definition.** Long-term ecological, economic, and social viability of
the food system — environmental stewardship, resource efficiency,
waste reduction, and the system's ability to operate indefinitely
without degrading the conditions it depends on.

**At HARVEST this means.** Food waste diversion, composting, food
miles, the environmental footprint of operations, and the financial
sustainability that lets HARVEST keep operating (earned revenue,
leveraged capital).

**Typical KPI tags.**
- Food waste diversion (lbs)
- Composted lbs
- Local food miles
- Food recovery to service providers (lbs)
- % earned revenue
- $ in leveraged capital

---

## Tagging guidance

When tagging a new KPI, ask the questions in order:

1. **Availability** — does this measure *whether food exists*?
2. **Access** — does this measure *whether people can get to it*?
3. **Utilization** — does this measure *how well it nourishes*?
4. **Stability** — does this measure *durability over time*?
5. **Agency** — does this measure *who decides*?
6. **Sustainability** — does this measure *long-term viability*?

Tag every dimension where the answer is a clear yes. Don't force-fit.
Most HARVEST KPIs land on 1–3 dimensions; a few (kitchen rental hours,
ServSafe training) span 4+.

## Bridge to the logic model

The OFSA template will ask for inputs → outputs → outcomes → impact
organized by dimension. The dimension tag on each KPI is what makes
that mapping mechanical instead of a redesign. When the logic model
phase starts, the **Dashboard — By OFSA Dimension** tab in the master
workbook is the starting point — every dimension's KPIs are already
grouped and quantified.
